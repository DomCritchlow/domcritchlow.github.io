About

I build technology that helps people make better decisions when in critical environments.

At Booz Allen, I lead applied AI and mission-software work across Europe and Africa, designing systems that connect data, people, and operations for defense and critical-infrastructure clients.

I started out studying physics and computer science at a small university in Tennessee, where I launched weather balloons and wrote Python scripts to chase them through the sky. That mix of curiosity and practicality, building something, testing it, and improving it, still shapes how I work today.

Since then I have built software for NASA and the Department of Defense, led engineering teams, and spent a lot of time thinking about how humans and machines learn to trust each other.

Outside of work I like to experiment. I build web tools, play with data visualizations, mess with radios, and chase side projects that start as a simple question and turn into something useful or just fun. I think good technology should solve real problems and make people a little more curious along the way.
