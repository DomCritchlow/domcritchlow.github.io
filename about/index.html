About

Dominic Critchlow is an artificial intelligence practitioner and technology leader whose work focuses on how organizations build, deploy, and operationalize AI systems. He currently works in forward-deployed AI in New York, building production AI products for enterprise users and helping organizations translate emerging model capabilities into durable software systems.

Dominic previously spent nearly a decade working at the intersection of technology and national security. At the U.S. Department of Defense’s Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office, he co-founded Gamechanger, an AI-powered platform for navigating defense policy and requirements. The effort grew from an early product he helped build into a 25-person team and an enterprise capability indexing millions of policy documents for users across the Department of Defense.

He later served as Chief Scientist for Booz Allen Hamilton in Europe, where he led a 60-person engineering organization developing AI-enabled software, analytics, and decision-support capabilities for U.S. European Command and U.S. Africa Command. His work has spanned product development, machine learning, data platforms, technical strategy, and the organizational challenges involved in moving emerging technologies from prototype to operational use.

His broader interests include AI-enabled institutions, government software, human-computer collaboration, technical organizations, and the changing economics of software development. He writes and speaks about AI adoption, software delivery in government, and how institutions can become more capable builders and users of modern technology.

Dominic is also an adjunct faculty member at Seton Hall University’s Stillman School of Business, where he teaches graduate Big Data Analytics, and serves as a Military Intelligence officer in the U.S. Army Reserve. He maintains an active technical practice through independent and open-source projects spanning machine learning, AI automation, data visualization, native applications, and radio systems. He studied physics and computer science at Austin Peay State University and earned a master’s degree in computer science from George Mason University.

Outside of work I like to experiment: iOS apps, data visualizations, and radio + machine learning projects that start as a simple question and turn into something useful, or at least fun. A recent one is Flags, which tracks how often every country is mentioned across bills, nominations, amendments, and the Congressional Record.

The best way to reach me is LinkedIn. You can also find me on GitHub.
